# Model Bundles Within the Three-Layer Catalog and Component Fulfillment

A Bundle remains inside the existing three-layer catalog: the Bundle is the Productable definition, Product is its storefront shell, and a composite Product Delivery Option is the purchasable SKU. The parent option is stored as `fulfillment_type = composite` and `delivery_method = bundle`; it has no direct provider delivery, Enrollment, access block, or independent Order Item. Bundle option creation stays in the normal Product and delivery-option administrative workflow, writing composition atomically through the delivery-option DTO without creating a parallel purchasable backend abstraction. The server rejects provider fields on the parent option, keeping `ims_course_code`, `schedule_days`, and `duration` null. For customer discovery, the catalog pairs this standard administrative model with dedicated PLP and PDP storefront endpoints that present composite fulfillment details. A Bundle Product may expose multiple fixed composite options, may span school departments under a single owning department, and allows draft Bundles to reference unpublished components while requiring published components for active sale.

Each composite option owns a fixed set of component Product Delivery Options and their price allocations, with every component appearing exactly once. A priced component may be allocated zero, but no allocation may exceed its base price. Public Bundle pricing exposes the sum of component base prices, the sum of allocated paid amounts, and the resulting customer savings. Bundle pricing is strictly independent of component promotions and cannot receive additional coupons, featured prices, or automatic promotions, though unrelated standalone items in the cart may still receive their own discounts. Active standalone discounts never alter Bundle allocations, material component changes require Bundle review, and referenced components cannot be hard-deleted. A component base-price change automatically removes affected Bundle options from sale until an administrator reviews their pricing. Purchase eligibility is governed by the underlying Productable rather than the delivery option: owning any component prevents purchasing the Bundle, purchasing the Bundle prevents subsequent standalone purchase of its components, nested Bundles are prohibited, and Bundle quantity is always one.

Carts validate the latest published composition, require full payment, and atomically reserve capacity across all components. Checkout creates one immutable Bundle Purchase and internal component Order Items, preserving the existing one-Order-Item/one-PDO/one-Enrollment provisioning and accounting seam while presenting and refunding the Bundle as one commercial unit. Orders snapshot this composition immutably. Bundle Component Order Items function strictly as internal fulfillment and accounting records: ordinary individual item refunds reject them, and customer order responses expose them exclusively beneath their parent Bundle Purchase rather than in the flat order items list.

Component Product Delivery Options remain the sole source of actual fulfillment, access, capacity, progress, certificates, and provider behavior across platforms such as Moodle, SpotPlayer, IMS, and digital files. When provisioning IMS components, IMS receives each component's allocated paid amount as actual payment and the difference from its base price as a manual discount, with the Bundle identity recorded in the enrollment note. Overall Bundle Purchase status is derived dynamically from all component enrollment and revocation states. When Bundle definitions or options are retired, historical records are archived rather than hard-deleted.

The Bundle Purchase is the indivisible commercial refund boundary. Refunds are initiated for the entire Bundle Purchase, apply its immutable checkout allocation snapshot, and target all and only its grouped component order items. Percentage deductions are calculated from undiscounted base value, while refundable money is bounded by the amount actually allocated and paid. The Bundle-level policy deduction is distributed across components by base-price weight, capped by paid allocation; shortfalls from free or insufficiently allocated components are redistributed across paid components with remaining value until the full policy deduction is assigned or paid value is exhausted. Full-order refunds compose Bundle refunds with unrelated standalone items into one combined gateway refund. Financial refund completion precedes asynchronous provider revocation, and the response exposes a single commercial summary alongside a component-level audit breakdown. Refunds revoke access across every component where supported; failed revocations leave the order and Bundle Purchase in a pending state with component-level retry visibility, requiring manual follow-up where unsupported and blocking repurchase until all component access is successfully revoked.
